I am a theoretical nuclear physicist and hold a permanent position as Professor of Physics at the Federal Center for Technological Education of Minas Gerais (CEFET-MG), Brazil, where I have been a faculty member since 2014.

I received my B.Sc. in Physics from the Federal University of Itajubá (UNIFEI) in 2010, followed by an M.Sc. (2012) and a Ph.D. (2016) in Physics from the Federal University of Santa Catarina (UFSC).

My research lies at the intersection of nuclear physics, particle physics, and astrophysics, with a particular emphasis on the properties of dense nuclear matter and the structure of compact stars. I am especially interested in understanding how microscopic strong interactions influence macroscopic astrophysical observables.

My work focuses on relativistic descriptions of nuclear matter, particularly Quantum Hadrodynamics (QHD) and relativistic mean-field models. I investigate nuclear equations of state, the role of exotic degrees of freedom such as hyperons, delta resonances, kaons, and deconfined quarks, and their implications for neutron-star properties. More recently, I have begun studying finite nuclei and the physics of neutron-star crusts.

I am a Research Productivity Fellow of the Brazilian National Council for Scientific and Technological Development (CNPq).

I welcome scientific collaborations in theoretical nuclear physics, dense matter, and compact-star astrophysics.
